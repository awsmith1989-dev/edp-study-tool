Exam Overview

What the EDP covers and how it's assessed

Study Tool Project Plan

The EDP has two parts, scored separately, and they reward different things. The multiple choice section tests what the manuals say. The oral exam tests whether you can apply it — and, in IEDC's own words, it is where experience counts the most. You have to pass the written portion before you're allowed to sit for the oral.

Part one

The multiple choice exam

One hundred questions in two hours and fifteen minutes — about eighty seconds each. Questions come directly from the IEDC training manuals.

The weighting is flat. Every manual contributes exactly twenty questions regardless of its length. The Credit Analysis manual is 213 pages and ELED II is 108 — both are worth the same twenty points. Study time should follow the exam structure, not page count.

International business is the one to watch. It's the only domain with no weak spot in any manual — it appears in BRE as a retention strategy, in Entre as both business plan development and small business export assistance, and in Credit as export financing and foreign investor due diligence. You can't prepare for it by reading one chapter, and the right answer often depends on which context the question is set in.

What IEDC says about preparing

Exam takers should focus on material that is expected to be known by an entrepreneurship development professional and are encouraged not to memorize the minutia of the manuals. IEDC EDP Application Booklet, 2026

Read that as guidance about emphasis rather than permission to skip detail. Specific figures do appear — the terms of an export credit insurance policy, which federal department runs a given program — but the majority of questions ask you to choose the right tool for a described situation.

Part two

The project assessment & oral exam

Once you've passed the written section, you sit for a formal oral examination before a panel of three EDP-certified graders. You submit a project plan in advance using IEDC's ELED Project Plan template, and the panel works from it — but not only from it.

What the panel is actually assessing

Graders review your plan and give feedback on it, then move well beyond it. Expect questions in four areas:

  • Your plan itself — why you scoped it that way, how you'd fund it, how you'd know it worked
  • Entrepreneurs your plan doesn't serve — if you named aspiring and startup founders, expect to be asked how second-stage or innovation-led firms are supported in your community
  • Problems facing entrepreneurs where you work — what the real barriers are, and what you are personally doing about them
  • Areas outside your expertise — trends and current practice across the field, not just your own lane

Four themes carry disproportionate weight: collaboration, coalitions, equity, and accessibility. Strong candidates thread these through every answer rather than treating them as one topic among many. A plan that opens by convening a diverse coalition before building anything signals all four at once.

What separates a strong plan

  • Scope it to something real. The strongest objectives extend an existing asset or close a specific named gap. "Build an entrepreneurial ecosystem" is not an objective.
  • Name things. Specific partner organizations, real figures, actual data sources you can access. Vagueness is the most common weakness.
  • Be honest about gaps. Writing "TBD" for an unresolved partner, or noting a step is already underway, reads as competence. Inventing diversified funding that doesn't exist reads as naive.
  • Thread the sections together. The same action steps should recur in your funding table and your timeline. Metrics that don't measure your stated objective is the single biggest tell of a weak plan.
  • Use metrics you can actually collect. Engagement and referral counts you track now beat job-creation figures that take years to attribute.
  • Include policy and advocacy. ELED treats policy as ecosystem infrastructure. Most candidates skip it entirely, which is exactly why including it stands out.
Although questions may focus on entrepreneurship-led economic development, it is during this section that experience counts the most. IEDC EDP Application Booklet, 2026

Both sections are pass-or-retake rather than scored. If you don't pass the oral, you can request grader feedback through IEDC — but never contact the graders directly.
